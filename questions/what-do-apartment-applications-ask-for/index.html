What Do Apartment Applications Ask For?

Apartment applications are standardized forms used by landlords and property managers to evaluate a prospective tenant's financial stability, rental history, and reliability. They typically ask for personal identification, income verification, employment details, past landlord references, and consent to run credit and background checks.

Published 10 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

Proof of Income and Employment Details

Property managers want to confirm you can afford the monthly rent reliably. Most require your gross monthly income to equal at least three times the rent amount. You will need to list your current employer, supervisor contact information, job title, and length of employment.

Gather your recent pay stubs covering the last thirty to sixty days. If you work for yourself, freelance, or run a small business, landlords ask for different documentation instead of standard pay stubs. You must prepare signed tax returns from the previous year, profit and loss statements, and bank statements showing consistent cash flow. Keep these files organized digitally so you can upload them quickly when you submit your rental application online.

If your income comes from investments, alimony, social security, or disability benefits, you must provide official award letters or account statements. Having these documents ready prevents delays in the review process, especially in competitive rental markets where multiple applicants apply for the same unit.

  • Recent pay stubs or earnings statements
  • Signed federal tax returns for self-employed applicants
  • Official bank statements showing steady deposits
  • W-2 forms from the previous calendar year

Personal Identification and Background Checks

Landlords verify your identity to protect their property and comply with federal housing regulations. You must provide a government-issued photo identification card, such as a state driver license, passport, or military ID. You will also need to supply your social security number or individual taxpayer identification number for screening purposes.

The application form asks for your current residential address, your previous addresses covering the last two to five years, and the contact details for your current landlord. Property managers contact past landlords to check if you paid rent on time, respected property rules, and provided proper notice before moving out.

Expect to pay a non-refundable application fee that covers the direct cost of pulling your credit report and criminal background check. Landlords look at your overall credit history, open collection accounts, and eviction records. If you have past credit errors or mistakes on your file, you can prepare a brief letter of explanation to give context to the landlord before they review the report.

  • State driver license or valid passport
  • Social security number for screening
  • Contact information for past landlords
  • Consent signature for credit and criminal history checks

Co-Signers, Guarantors, and References

If your income falls below the required threshold, or if you lack an established credit history, the application allows for a guarantor or co-signer. A guarantor promises to pay the rent if you default on the lease agreement. They must fill out a separate application form and undergo the same financial screening.

Personal references who are not related to you are sometimes requested to vouch for your character and reliability. Choose former colleagues, mentors, or long-term acquaintances who can speak positively about your responsibility. Prepare their phone numbers and email addresses ahead of time.

Pet owners must provide additional details about their animals. Landlords ask for the pet type, breed, weight, age, and vaccination records. Some properties also require a photograph of the pet and references from a previous landlord regarding the animal's behavior.

How to Prepare Your Application Package

Organization is the key to getting approved quickly. Create a digital folder containing scanned copies of your identification, tax documents, pay stubs, and reference list. When you tour a property, you can submit your complete packet immediately.

Review your credit report before applying to spot any inaccurate items. If you find mistakes, remember that disputing an error is free and that you send your own letters directly to the credit bureaus. Resolving issues early prevents surprises when the landlord runs their check.

Be honest on every line of the form. Misstating facts or omitting eviction history on a rental application gives the landlord grounds to deny your tenancy or terminate a signed lease later. Clear, truthful, and complete paperwork shows you are a dependable tenant.

What the sources say

  • The Fair Credit Reporting Act regulates how consumer reporting agencies use and share credit information for tenant screening. (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau)
  • Landlords must comply with the federal Fair Housing Act, which prohibits discrimination in housing based on race, color, religion, sex, familial status, national origin, or disability. (U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development)

Frequently asked questions

What credit score is needed to rent an apartment?

Credit score requirements vary by property, city, and landlord discretion. Many apartment complexes look for a score in the good range, while private landlords may be more flexible if you offer a higher security deposit or provide a qualified guarantor.

Can a landlord charge an application fee?

Yes, landlords typically charge an application fee to cover the out-of-pocket costs of running credit and background checks. State laws often regulate the maximum amount a landlord can charge for these screening reports.

What happens if I have no credit history?

Having no credit history means landlords cannot evaluate your past borrowing habits. You can overcome this by providing proof of steady savings, offering a larger security deposit, paying several months of rent in advance, or using a credit-approved co-signer.

Do all landlords require proof of income?

Almost all professional property management companies and landlords require formal proof of income to ensure you can meet the monthly financial obligation. They typically verify this by requesting pay stubs, tax documents, or employer contact verification.

Letter Confirming Employment Template

A letter confirming employment is a formal document provided by an employer that verifies an individual's current job title, employment status, length of service, and compensation details. Lenders, landlords, and leasing agents request this verification letter to confirm income stability and employment history during application reviews.

Published 3 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

What should an employment confirmation template include?

A standard letter confirming employment should include specific information to satisfy the requirements of a mortgage lender, landlord, or credit issuer. The document must always be printed on official company letterhead to establish authenticity and credibility. It should start with the date of issuance and the direct contact information of the human resources department or the manager writing the letter.

The body of the letter needs to state the employee's full legal name, current job title, and whether the position is classified as full-time, part-time, or seasonal. You should include the exact start date of employment to verify stability. Compensation details are critical for financial applications, so the letter must state the current salary or hourly wage, along with the average number of hours worked per week if the employee is hourly or works part-time. Mentioning bonus or commission structures can also help if those earnings form part of your total qualifying income.

Closing the letter requires the physical signature or digital equivalent of an authorized company representative, such as a human resources manager or business owner, alongside their printed name and title. Providing a reliable phone number or email address allows the requesting party to perform a standard secondary verification if needed. Keeping the layout clean and professional ensures that the reviewer can quickly locate all necessary data points without confusion.

When do you need a letter confirming employment?

You will encounter requests for employment confirmation during major life transitions and financial transactions. Applying for a residential lease is one of the most common situations where a landlord or property management company asks for proof of income to ensure you can comfortably afford the monthly rent. They use this document to cross-reference your stated salary against the rent-to-income ratio guidelines they enforce for all tenants.

Mortgage applications and home purchases also demand strict verification of your employment and income history. Underwriters look for a stable two-year work history, especially if your income includes variable components like overtime, tips, or commissions. Providing a detailed confirmation letter helps the underwriter trace your income streams and verify that your position remains secure while the loan underwriting process moves toward final approval.

Beyond housing, various credit applications, personal loans, and professional background checks may require official employment proof. Self-employed individuals typically provide different documents, such as tax returns and profit-and-loss statements, but W-2 employees rely heavily on employer-generated letters. Preparing this paperwork ahead of time prevents delays in your application timeline and keeps your approval process moving forward smoothly.

How to format your employment verification letter

Formatting your confirmation letter correctly ensures that third-party reviewers take the document seriously. The top section must feature the employer's business letterhead, including the company name, corporate address, telephone number, and official website or email domain. Avoid plain white paper without letterhead, as unverified documents often trigger rejection or requests for secondary confirmation.

The tone of the letter should remain strictly professional, objective, and factual. Avoid adding subjective praise, personal opinions, or speculative statements about the future of the employee at the company. Stick strictly to verified personnel records regarding dates, titles, and earnings. If the letter is meant for a specific recipient, such as a particular apartment complex or mortgage broker, address it directly to them or use a general salutation like To Whom It May Concern if the recipient is unknown.

Tips for requesting your employment letter

Asking your employer for a confirmation letter requires polite communication and a clear explanation of your needs. Reach out to your human resources department or direct supervisor well in advance of your application deadline, as corporate offices may have internal protocols or bureaucratic delays that prevent same-day fulfillment.

Provide the HR representative with a checklist of the exact details the requesting party needs to see, such as your hourly rate, bonus history, or specific employment classification. Giving them a draft or template can significantly speed up the process because they only need to review the data, fill in the blanks, and print the document on company letterhead.

Always review the final letter thoroughly before submitting it to a landlord or lender. Check that your name is spelled correctly, your start date matches your personal records, and the contact information for your employer is up to date. Small typos or missing figures can cause the reviewer to pause your application while they request a corrected version.

Frequently asked questions

Should the letter be printed on company letterhead?

Yes, an employment confirmation letter must always be printed on official company letterhead to prove its authenticity. Landlords and lenders routinely reject letters submitted on plain paper because they cannot verify the true source of the income details.

How long is an employment verification letter valid?

Most verification letters remain valid for thirty to sixty days from the date of issuance. Reviewers prefer fresh documents because employment status, salary levels, and job titles can change rapidly in dynamic corporate environments.

Can I write my own employment confirmation letter?

You should never write and sign your own employment verification letter if you are an employee. An authorized representative of the company, such as an HR manager or business owner, must draft and sign the document to maintain legal accuracy.

What if my employer refuses to provide salary details?

If your employer has a strict policy against disclosing specific salary figures, you can provide alternative income proof such as recent pay stubs, tax returns, or bank statements alongside a basic verification letter confirming your job title and status.

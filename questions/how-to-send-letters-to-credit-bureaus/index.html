How to Send Letters to Credit Bureaus

Sending letters to credit bureaus is the self-service process of mailing written dispute notices about inaccurate items on your credit reports directly to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. You can complete this entire procedure entirely on your own without paying a monthly credit repair company.

Published 9 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

Why send letters yourself instead of paying a service

Many monthly credit repair companies charge ongoing subscription fees to mail the exact same dispute notices that you can draft and mail on your own for the cost of a postage stamp. Federal law grants every consumer the absolute right to contact nationwide consumer reporting agencies directly to challenge mistakes without hiring a middleman.

When you use a monthly credit repair service, your money goes toward administrative overhead rather than faster processing times. The major credit reporting agencies must investigate valid consumer disputes regardless of whether a third-party agency or you personally mailed the envelope. Taking charge of your own paperwork keeps your hard-earned money in your pocket while you work toward financial readiness for a mortgage, apartment, or loan application.

Disputing an error is free under federal law, meaning you never need to spend hundreds of dollars on subscription fees. What information to include in your mailing envelope

Your dispute package needs specific identifying details so the bureau can locate your file quickly and investigate your claim without unnecessary delays. Start with your full legal name, current residential address, date of birth, and social security number. Including a copy of a government-issued photo identification and a recent utility bill helps verify your identity and prevents processing holdups.

Clearly identify each disputed item by referencing the specific account name, account number, and the exact reason you believe the information is wrong. Keep your tone neutral, factual, and concise while explaining why the data reported does not match your personal records. Attach clear copies of any supporting documents that prove your case, but always keep your original paperwork safely at home.

  1. Full legal name and current home address
  2. Social security number and date of birth
  3. Specific account names and account numbers
  4. Clear copies of supporting paperwork

How to mail your documents safely

Mailing your paperwork requires careful attention to postal procedures to ensure your package arrives securely at the correct corporate processing center. Send your envelope through United States Postal Service Certified Mail with a return receipt requested so you have legal proof of delivery and receipt dates.

Keep a complete physical binder containing copies of every letter, envelope, identification document, and postal tracking receipt you generate during this process. Organized records protect your interests and provide a clear timeline should you need to follow up with the reporting agency or provide documentation to a future landlord or lender.

What happens after the bureau receives your letter

Once the consumer reporting agency receives your mailed dispute package, federal guidelines generally require them to complete an investigation within a standard timeframe. The agency must review the details you provided, forward your relevant information to the company that furnished the data, and record the results of the review.

When the investigation concludes, the agency must send you written notice of the outcome along with an updated copy of your file if any changes occurred. Remember that filing a dispute never guarantees a specific outcome, score adjustment, or the removal of accurate information from your file. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pay anything to mail my dispute letters?

You only pay the standard cost of postage and certified mail fees when sending letters yourself. Disputing an error is free under federal law, and you never need to pay a subscription fee to contact the bureaus.

Should I mail my original documents to the bureaus?

Never mail your original identification cards, birth certificates, or financial statements to any consumer reporting agency. Always send clear photocopies and keep your original paperwork safely stored in your home filing cabinet.

How long do credit bureaus take to reply?

Federal regulations generally require consumer reporting agencies to complete their review and mail a response within thirty days of receiving your correspondence. This window allows them time to contact the data furnisher and evaluate your claims thoroughly.

Can I dispute information with multiple agencies at once?

Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion operate as separate independent entities that do not share dispute files with each other. If inaccurate data appears across multiple reports, you must send separate notification packages to each agency individually.

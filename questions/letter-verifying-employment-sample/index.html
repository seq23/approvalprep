How to write a letter verifying employment sample

An employment verification letter sample is a formal document provided by an employer that confirms a worker's job title, employment status, hire date, and current salary. Lenders, landlords, and background checkers use this letter to verify income and stability during an application process.

Published 2 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

What is an employment verification letter?

An employment verification letter is a written statement from an employer confirming your current job details. When you apply for an apartment lease, a mortgage, a car loan, or a new job, the reviewer often needs official proof of your income and employment status. This document serves as that proof.

The letter typically comes on company letterhead and includes essential facts about your position. It shows that you are actively employed, how long you have worked there, and how much you earn. Reviewers rely on this information to assess your financial reliability and your ability to make regular payments.

While some companies use automated third-party services to handle these requests, many small businesses, startups, and human resources departments still write these letters manually. Knowing what the letter should contain helps you request the correct information and ensures your application moves forward without unnecessary delays from missing details.

What details must appear in the letter?

A standard employment verification letter includes specific details to satisfy the requirements of a lender or landlord. First, the letter should state your official job title and whether your position is full-time, part-time, or seasonal. This establishes the nature of your work commitment.

Next, the document must list your exact hire date. Knowing your start date helps the reviewer calculate your employment history, which is especially important for mortgage applications that require a steady two-year work history. The letter should also state your current compensation, including your salary or hourly wage and average hours worked per week.

Finally, the letter needs to be printed on official company letterhead and signed by an authorized representative, such as a manager, human resources officer, or business owner. The contact information of the person signing should be included so the reviewer can follow up with any questions if necessary.

How to use an employment verification letter sample

Using a sample letter helps you understand the standard format and tone required for official requests. When you look at a sample, pay attention to the professional language and the clear arrangement of facts. You should never guess or exaggerate any details when preparing your own documents.

If you are self-employed or run your own small business, the process looks different because you cannot verify your own employment the same way a traditional employee does. In those cases, reviewers may ask for tax returns, profit and loss statements, or accountant letters instead of a standard employer letter. Always check the specific checklist provided by your landlord or lender.

Keep your requests simple and direct when asking your employer for this letter. Provide them with the exact details requested by the reviewer, such as your start date and salary, to make the process as easy as possible for your human resources department. Clear preparation prevents back-and-forth delays.

Common mistakes to avoid on verification letters

Errors on an employment verification letter can stall your mortgage or rental application. One common mistake is leaving out contact information for the employer, which prevents the reviewer from authenticating the document. Always ensure a phone number or email address is clearly visible on the letterhead or at the bottom of the page.

Another mistake is including outdated or incorrect salary figures. If you received a recent pay raise or switched from hourly to salaried status, make sure the letter reflects your current compensation rather than your starting pay. Discrepancies between your pay stubs and the verification letter will cause immediate questions.

Finally, do not alter a letter provided by your employer or attempt to write one yourself if you are not authorized to do so. Lenders and landlords verify employment directly with companies, and submitting unauthorized documents can result in an immediate rejection of your application.

Frequently asked questions

Can I write my own employment verification letter if my boss is busy?

No, you should never write your own employment verification letter or sign it on behalf of your employer. Lenders and landlords require verification letters to come directly from an authorized manager, human resources representative, or company owner to ensure the income and job details are completely accurate and authentic.

Does the letter need to state my reason for leaving a past job?

An employment verification letter only covers your current or past job details, such as your title, dates of employment, and salary. It generally does not need to state why you left a position unless a specific background check form explicitly asks for that information from a former employer.

What if my company has closed down and cannot write a letter?

If your former employer has gone out of business, you can provide alternative proof of past employment. Acceptable substitutes often include your W-2 tax forms from those years, historical tax returns, or bank statements showing direct deposits from the defunct company, along with your original job offer letter.

How long is an employment verification letter valid for applications?

Most lenders and landlords consider an employment verification letter valid for thirty to sixty days from the date it is signed. Because job status and salary can change quickly, reviewers usually request a freshly dated letter if your application process extends beyond that timeframe.

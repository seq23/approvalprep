What Is a Goodwill Credit Adjustment?

A goodwill credit adjustment is a direct written request asking a lender or creditor to remove a correct late payment mark from your credit reports as a courtesy. Creditors are never required to grant these requests, and they only consider them for accounts that are now current and in good standing.

Published 13 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

How a Goodwill Letter Differs From a Dispute

Understanding the difference between a dispute and a goodwill request matters before you contact any institution. When you file a dispute with a credit bureau, you claim that information reported by a data furnisher contains an error, mistake, or outdated detail. The bureau must investigate your claim under federal law and verify the facts within a set window of time.

A goodwill request operates under entirely different rules. You do not claim the information is wrong or inaccurate. Instead, you admit the late payment occurred as reported, explain the unusual life event that caused the temporary slip, and highlight your long history of on-time payments since that event. Because this is purely voluntary, the creditor holds total discretion over the final choice.

  • Disputes challenge inaccurate or unverified data.
  • Goodwill requests address accurate past payment lags.
  • Federal laws govern formal bureau dispute timelines.
  • Creditor policy alone governs goodwill decisions.

When Lenders Consider Goodwill Removals

Financial institutions rarely grant these requests for repeat offenders or accounts that remain past due. Most review boards look for a strong history of recent reliability before offering any form of courtesy correction. If your account has stayed current for twelve to twenty-four consecutive months following the single isolated incident, your odds improve slightly.

Common situations that prompt a favorable review include unexpected medical emergencies, temporary job loss, natural disasters, or clerical bank errors that caused an automated payment to fail. You must provide clear context about why the event happened and show proof that your current financial habits are stable and predictable.

What to Include in Your Written Request

Preparing your own letter requires clear details so the department reviewing your file can find your account immediately. Start by listing your full legal name, current address, phone number, and the exact account number associated with the late mark. Keep your tone polite, professional, and concise, avoiding emotional appeals or demands.

Clearly state the exact month and year of the late payment you want removed. Describe the temporary hardship that led to the delay without making excuses. Conclude by thanking the reviewer for their time and consideration, and mail the document to the corporate office or designated customer goodwill department.

Managing Expectations for Your Credit Profile

No rule or law forces a bank, credit union, or loan servicer to honor a goodwill adjustment request. Many major institutions maintain strict internal policies that prohibit the removal of accurate historical data to maintain the integrity of their reporting records.

If a creditor denies your request or simply ignores the letter, you must accept their decision and move forward. Focus your energy on maintaining ongoing positive payment habits, keeping your revolving balances low, and letting time gradually lessen the impact of older marks on your credit profile.

Frequently asked questions

Can I call instead of writing a letter?

Phone representatives rarely have the authority to alter historical credit data. Sending a written request to the designated department ensures your message reaches someone with the correct permissions to review account records.

Will a goodwill adjustment raise my score?

Removing an accurate late payment can sometimes improve your credit score, but results vary widely based on your overall profile. No outcome is guaranteed when dealing with voluntary creditor adjustments.

How long does a goodwill adjustment take to process?

Creditors do not operate under strict legal timelines for goodwill requests. Review periods often take anywhere from thirty to sixty days, and many institutions never reply if they decide to deny the adjustment.

Should I contact the credit bureaus for goodwill?

Credit bureaus only record the data furnished by your lenders and cannot grant goodwill adjustments. You must contact the original creditor or loan servicer that reported the late payment directly.

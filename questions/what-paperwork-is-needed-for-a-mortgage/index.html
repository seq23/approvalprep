What paperwork is needed for a mortgage?

A mortgage application paperwork checklist is the complete set of financial and personal documents required by a lender to evaluate your ability to repay a home loan. It typically includes proof of income, asset records, tax returns, and identification documents.

Published 3 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

What income documents do lenders require?

Lenders need to verify that you have a steady and reliable income to make your monthly mortgage payments. If you are a W-2 employee, you must provide your most recent pay stubs covering at least thirty consecutive days. You also need to submit your W-2 forms from the past two tax years. These documents show your base salary, overtime, bonuses, or commission earnings.

If you are self-employed, an independent contractor, or own a small business, the documentation requirements are more extensive. You will generally need to provide personal and business federal tax returns for the past two years, along with a year-to-date profit and loss statement and a current balance sheet. Lenders review these records to determine your stable net income after business expenses. Additional income sources, such as alimony, child support, or social security benefits, require official award letters and proof of regular receipt.

How do you prove your assets and funds for closing?

To qualify for a mortgage, you must demonstrate that you have enough money for the down payment and closing costs, as well as cash reserves left over after the purchase. Lenders request complete bank statements for all checking, savings, and money market accounts for the past two to three months. Every page of each statement must be included, even if some pages are blank, so the lender can review all account activity.

If you hold funds in investment accounts, retirement funds like a 401(k) or IRA, or certificates of deposit, you must provide statements for those accounts as well. Any large or unusual deposit appearing in your bank statements will require a written explanation and a paper trail, such as a gift letter if a relative provided the funds for your down payment.

What identification and personal history records are necessary?

You must prove your identity and legal residency status in the United States before a lender can process your loan application. Acceptable identification usually includes a valid government-issued photo ID, such as a driver license or passport. Permanent residents must provide a copy of their green card, while foreign nationals need appropriate visa documentation.

Lenders also collect personal history details, including your current residential address and your rental history or mortgage payment history for the past two years. You may need to provide landlord contact information or canceled rent checks. If you have experienced major credit events like bankruptcy or foreclosure in the past, gather the discharge papers and related legal court documents to attach to your file.

Why do lenders ask for a letter of explanation?

Underwriters review every detail of your financial profile and often spot inconsistencies or items that require clarification. A letter of explanation is a formal, signed document where you address specific questions raised by the lender regarding your credit report, employment gaps, address history, or financial accounts.

When writing an explanation letter, remain factual, clear, and concise. State the exact reason for the inquiry, such as a temporary job transition or a disputed inquiry on your credit report, and provide relevant dates. Never misstate facts or omit required details, as lenders verify all provided information against official public records and third-party databases before final loan approval.

Frequently asked questions

How many months of bank statements do I need for a mortgage?

Lenders typically require two to three months of complete bank statements for all checking, savings, and investment accounts. You must include every page of each statement to show your account balances and transaction history clearly.

Can I use gift funds for my mortgage down payment?

Yes, you can use gift funds from a family member, but the lender requires a signed gift letter. The letter must state that the money is a true gift with no expectation of repayment, along with proof of transfer.

What happens if I have an employment gap in my history?

An employment gap of more than a month usually requires a written explanation. Lenders want to see that you have stable current employment and that the reason for the past gap has been fully resolved.

Do I need to provide tax returns if I am a salaried employee?

Salaried employees generally do not need to provide personal tax returns unless they earn significant income from commissions, tips, or rental properties, or if they own a portion of a business.

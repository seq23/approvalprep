How to dispute a credit report error yourself

Disputing a credit report error is the process of notifying nationwide consumer reporting agencies or data furnishers in writing about incorrect information on your credit history file, allowing them to investigate, correct, or remove inaccurate data according to federal law without paying a monthly credit repair subscription fee.

Published 16 November 2026 · Updated 27 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

Checking your credit reports for mistakes

Before you can challenge an inaccuracy, you need to review your credit files from the major reporting agencies. Federal law gives you the right to access these files to look for mistakes, outdated accounts, or unfamiliar debts. Check your personal details, such as your name, previous addresses, and employment records, because identity mix-ups frequently create inaccurate entries that impact your credit standing.

Carefully examine every trade line listed on your report. Look for balances that appear incorrect, payment histories marked late when you paid on time, or accounts that do not belong to you. When you spot a discrepancy, gather your personal records, bank statements, or payment receipts to back up your claim. You must never attempt to dispute accurate negative information or misstate facts to a credit bureau, as agencies only investigate items you believe are genuinely wrong.

  1. Order your official reports from the centralized federal website
  2. Review all personal identifying information for current accuracy
  3. Identify specific trade lines that contain incorrect details
  4. Gather supporting documents such as canceled checks or statements

Writing your own credit dispute letter

You have the legal right to act as your own advocate by writing and mailing your own dispute letters directly to the credit bureaus. Many commercial services charge ongoing monthly fees for this exact task, but you can do it completely free of charge. Your letter should clearly identify the specific item you are challenging, explain why the information is incorrect, and state what outcome you are requesting, such as a correction or deletion of the inaccurate entry.

Sending your documents to the bureau

Once you finish drafting your correspondence, mail the package to the specific bureau that reported the inaccurate data. Each nationwide agency maintains a designated address for consumer disputes, which you can find on your credit report or their official web portal. Consider using certified mail with a return receipt requested so you have proof of the date the agency received your paperwork.

Under federal regulations, the bureau must log your request and forward your details to the company that furnished the information. The reporting agency keeps a record of your communication and initiates an inquiry into the disputed item. Make sure your return address is current so the agency can mail its written response directly to your home once the review concludes.

  1. Mail your package to the correct bureau dispute address
  2. Request delivery confirmation for tracking purposes
  3. Include clear copies of your evidence
  4. Keep complete records of every mailing receipt

What happens during the bureau investigation

After receiving your written notice, the consumer reporting agency generally has a set timeframe under federal rules to complete its investigation. During this period, the bureau contacts the creditor or data furnisher that supplied the disputed information, providing them with all the details and evidence you submitted in your letter.

The data furnisher must review the material, check its internal records, and report back to the bureau. If the furnisher finds that the data is indeed inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable, it must notify all nationwide bureaus to update or delete the item. Once the review wraps up, the bureau sends you a written notice detailing the final results of their investigation.

How to write a credit dispute letter

A clear credit dispute letter identifies your identity, the specific item you are challenging, and the reason for the correction. Begin by including your full legal name, current address, date of birth, Social Security Number, and a copy of a government-issued ID and utility bill to verify your identity. Clearly reference the account name, account number, and the exact reason for the dispute, such as an incorrect balance, a duplicate listing, or an account that does not belong to you. State what correction or deletion you are requesting from the credit reporting agency. Attach copies of supporting documents that prove your claim, such as canceled checks, monthly statements, or a letter from the creditor acknowledging the error. Never send original documents. Conclude with a polite closing, your signature, and the date. Keep copies of everything you send and use a traceable mailing method to confirm delivery.

How long a credit report dispute takes to resolve

Credit reporting agencies generally have thirty days from the date they receive your dispute to complete their investigation. In certain situations where you submit additional information during the initial thirty-day window, the agency may receive an extension of up to fifteen days, making the total possible timeframe forty-five days. Once the investigation is complete, the bureau must provide you with the written results within five business days. If the furnisher of the information updates or deletes the item based on your evidence, the bureau must also send you a revised copy of your credit report showing the update. If the dispute does not result in a change, the bureau must explain why and inform you of your right to add a statement of dispute to your credit file. Remember that investigating errors takes time, and complex issues involving multiple creditors can require patient follow-up over several weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay to dispute credit report errors?

No law requires you to pay anyone to challenge mistakes on your credit reports. You possess the legal right to investigate errors and mail your own letters entirely for free without hiring a commercial service.

How long do credit bureaus take to investigate?

Federal regulations typically require nationwide consumer reporting agencies to complete their reviews and mail their findings to you within thirty to forty-five days after they receive your initial written correspondence.

Can I dispute accurate negative information?

You cannot successfully challenge accurate and verifiable negative information through the dispute process. Federal law only permits investigations into entries that you have a reasonable basis to believe contain factual errors or outdated data.

What happens after the investigation finishes?

The reporting agency must mail you a written summary of the results once the review concludes. If they correct an item, they will also provide you with an updated copy of your credit report showing the changes.

How long does a credit bureau have to investigate my dispute?

Under federal law, credit reporting agencies typically have 30 days to investigate your dispute after receiving your letter and documents. This period can sometimes extend to 45 days if you provide additional information during the initial review window. The bureau must mail you written results within five business days of completing the investigation.

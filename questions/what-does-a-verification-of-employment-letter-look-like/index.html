What Does a Verification of Employment Letter Look Like?

A verification of employment letter is a formal document printed on company letterhead that states an employee's hire date, current job title, employment status, and earnings. It typically follows a standard business letter format, starting with the employer's contact details, addressing the recipient directly, and concluding with an authorized manager signature.

Published 3 November 2026 · ApprovalPrep

What is the standard layout of an employment verification letter?

A standard verification of employment letter follows professional business correspondence guidelines. It always begins with the official company letterhead at the top of the page. This letterhead includes the business name, physical address, phone number, and official website or email contact. If the company does not use letterhead, the human resources department usually places the document on standard business stationery and stamps it with the corporate seal.

Below the letterhead, the document shows the date the letter was drafted. Next comes the recipient information, which often names the specific mortgage lender, landlord, or agency requesting the data. The salutation addresses the reader professionally, such as To Whom It May Concern or Dear Loan Officer. The body of the letter spans one or two concise paragraphs that state the necessary facts about your employment history without adding unnecessary commentary or subjective praise.

The document closes with a formal sign-off, such as Sincerely, followed by the handwritten or digital signature of the company representative. Below the signature, the writer types their full legal name, official title within the human resources or payroll department, and direct contact phone number. This closing block allows the requesting party to verify the authenticity of the letter with a quick phone call.

What specific details appear in the body of the letter?

The body paragraphs contain exact facts about your professional standing with the company. The text starts by confirming your official job title and whether your position is classified as full-time, part-time, seasonal, or temporary. It explicitly states your exact start date with the organization. If you received promotions, the letter may list your original hire date alongside the date you transitioned into your current role.

Compensation details form a major part of the document. The letter states your current rate of pay, whether you earn an hourly wage, a salaried amount, or commission-based income. It often breaks down your earnings into gross figures calculated on a weekly, monthly, or annual basis. For workers with fluctuating hours, the text might include average overtime or bonus earnings received over the past year.

Finally, the letter addresses your current work status. It confirms whether you are actively on the job, on an approved leave of absence, or recently separated from the company. The text avoids offering personal opinions about your work ethic or character, sticking strictly to verifiable administrative facts found in your personnel file.

Who is authorized to write and sign this document?

Only designated personnel hold the authority to issue an official employment verification letter. Typically, this task falls to human resources managers, payroll specialists, or company owners in smaller businesses. Direct supervisors sometimes write these letters, but lenders and landlords often prefer human resources or payroll staff because they manage official company records.

The person signing the letter must have access to your official personnel file to confirm that every statement is accurate. Lenders and property managers check the signature against corporate directories. If a coworker writes the letter or if you draft it yourself without authorization, the requesting institution will reject the document and ask for an official reissue from management.

When requesting this document from your employer, give them clear instructions on what the recipient needs. Providing a precise list of required data points saves time and prevents back-and-forth communication between your employer and the third party. Remind your HR department to use official company contact details so the lender can complete their routine verification call without delay.

Self-employed individuals cannot write their own employment verification letters in this manner. If you run your own business, verification usually relies on tax returns, profit and loss statements, and accountant letters rather than a standard corporate employment letter. Always verify what specific proofs your lender or landlord accepts before submitting self-employment paperwork.

Why do lenders and landlords inspect this format closely?

Lenders and landlords inspect employment verification letters closely to protect against fraud and ensure your income supports your financial obligations. A properly formatted letter on genuine company letterhead provides immediate confidence that your income claims are authentic. Underwriters look for inconsistencies, such as altered fonts, missing contact details, or vague earnings descriptions that make verification difficult.

Financial institutions cross-reference the details on the letter against your bank statements, W-2 forms, and pay stubs. If the job title or salary on the letter does not match your tax documents, the lender stops the review process to investigate the discrepancy. Clear, standardized formatting speeds up this review because loan officers can spot necessary data points instantly.

Property managers also use these letters to confirm job stability. Knowing your hire date and employment status helps landlords assess your long-term ability to pay rent. A professional layout communicates that you maintain a stable relationship with a legitimate employer, which strengthens your rental or loan application significantly.

Frequently asked questions

Does the letter need to be notarized?

Employment verification letters rarely require notarization because lenders and landlords rely on direct phone calls or email confirmations with the human resources department instead. A signed letter on official company letterhead with direct contact information is normally sufficient for any standard application review process.

How long is an employment verification letter valid?

Most lenders and landlords consider an employment verification letter valid for thirty to sixty days from the date of issuance. Because job status and compensation can change quickly, financial institutions usually require a freshly signed document if your application process extends beyond that timeframe.

Can my employer refuse to write the letter?

Employers are generally not legally required to provide a detailed verification letter beyond confirming basic employment dates and job titles. However, most human resources departments routinely supply standard verification letters to support their employees with mortgage, loan, or apartment applications upon formal request.

What happens if my company has no formal letterhead?

If your employer lacks official letterhead, the human resources representative should type the verification on plain business stationery and attach a business card. The letter must still include complete corporate contact details, a clear manager signature, and verifiable phone numbers so the requesting party can authenticate the details.
